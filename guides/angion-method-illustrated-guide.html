Read this first: safety

This is an exercise and self-monitoring guide, not medical advice. None of the techniques here has been tested in a clinical trial.

Go to emergency care right away if

  • An erection lasts more than 4 hours (priapism). It can permanently damage erectile tissue. The source calls short post-session episodes "priapisms"; engorgement that fades on its own is not an emergency, but anything approaching 4 hours is.
  • You hear or feel a pop or crack and then get sudden loss of erection, pain, rapid swelling or dark bruising. This can be a penile fracture, which usually needs surgery.
  • You see blood in your urine or can't urinate.
  • If you take PDE5 inhibitors (sildenafil, tadalafil): sudden vision loss in one or both eyes.

Stop the session (and see a doctor if it persists) if

  • You feel pain or tenderness. The source's own rule is "pleasure, not pain."
  • You notice red pinpoint spots (petechiae), blood blisters or bruising.
  • Numbness or tingling lasts after the session.
  • You notice a new lump, curve, dent or bend in the shaft (a possible sign of Peyronie's disease).
  • You get pain in the groin or testicles. First check that you are lying down (see §05).
  • Erection quality drops for several days in a row (overtraining, §16).

See a doctor before you start if

  • You have any erection problems. Erectile dysfunction (ED) is often an early warning sign of heart disease, diabetes, high blood pressure, high cholesterol or low testosterone, and can show up years before other symptoms (Thompson et al., JAMA 2005). The training below does not replace that check-up.
  • You can't get an erection without medication or a device. The source itself recommends a penile Doppler ultrasound to check the arteries for blockage or calcification before Stage E.
  • You have Peyronie's disease or curvature, a past penile injury or surgery, a bleeding disorder, sickle-cell disease, or you take blood thinners.
  • You take nitrates, blood-pressure medication or PDE5 inhibitors and are considering the L-arginine or L-citrulline supplements mentioned in the source.

The source's ground rules

Rule 1

Pleasure, not pain

"This is an exercise, it should be very pleasurable. Any pain is a bad thing."

Rule 2

Always lie down

Never train seated. Nearly all reported groin and testicle pain went away once men switched to lying on their back.

Rule 3

Lube when specified

AM1, AM2.5 and AM3 use lubricant. AM2 and the Wheel are done dry.

Rule 4

Ramp up slowly

Start each session slow and speed up only as the tissue fills. The creator ruptured small vessels by speeding up too fast.

Rule 5

Rest is training

Train 1-on / 1-off, or 1-on / 2-off. Vessels remodel on the days between sessions.

Rule 6

Don't mix methods

Don't combine with clamps, constriction bands, hanging or classic jelqing (the source's one exception is the brief hand clamp in BFR, §15), and never train under anything that dulls pain.

What the Angion Method is

Blood-flow training for the penis: "cardio for the member," done as a progressive program.

The Angion Method is a set of hand techniques, later extended with devices, developed by Janus Bifrons. Its first goal is better erection quality (EQ). Its long-term goal is size. It tries to get there by repeatedly raising blood flow through specific vessels of the penis, in timed sessions, with rest days in between, the way a runner trains the heart and legs.

Where it came from

In the podcast interview in our compiled material, Janus describes coming from competitive bodybuilding, then spending years on traditional enlargement methods: jelqing, stretching, pumping, extenders, self-sourced injections and PDE5 inhibitors. By his mid-20s, he says, he had severe ED. At that point he changed his goal from "bigger" to "functional again" and started studying vascular biology, in particular arteriogenesis, the growth of arteries driven by the friction of blood flow (shear stress). He designed hand techniques to raise flow through the penis's own vessels. He reports getting function back first and then noticing growth, which he later named phallogenesis.

Foundations

Anatomy you need

Three chambers, two wrappings and one blood "circuit." Every technique later in the guide targets a specific structure on this page.

Rigidity

Corpora cavernosa (CC)

Two chambers side by side on top. Each has a cavernosal (deep) artery down its center that branches into helicine arteries, which fill sponge-like spaces called sinusoids. They are wrapped in the thick tunica albuginea. Community estimate: about 85–90% of erect volume.

Underside

Corpus spongiosum (CS)

One chamber along the underside, around the urethra. It has a thin tunica, so it stays compressible even during an erection, which keeps the urethra open. At the tip it expands to form the glans. The community treats CS and glans as one structure.

Tip

Glans

No tunica albuginea, so it is elastic. In the source's terms it fills, flares and discharges like a "pressure buffer." Its blood drains mainly into the deep dorsal vein on top.

Wrappings

Buck's & dartos fascia

Buck's fascia (deep) wraps all three chambers along with the deep dorsal vein, dorsal arteries and nerves. The dartos/Colles' fascia (superficial) lies under the skin around the superficial dorsal vein.

Inside the body

Roots & pelvic floor

The CC roots (crura) anchor to the pubic bones under the ischiocavernosus (IC) muscles. The CS bulb sits under the bulbospongiosus (BS/BC). A large share of erectile tissue is under the skin of the perineum, out of sight.

Caution zone

Dorsal nerves

The main sensory nerves run along the top of the shaft next to the DDV. Firm thumb pressure there (AM1) is fine. Heavy, repeated pressure or devices are not.

The supply line

The "bulbo-dorsal circuit": the loop every Angion technique works

Evidence check: anatomy

  • Established The three-chamber structure, tunica albuginea, Buck's fascia and the pudendal → bulbourethral / cavernosal / dorsal branching all match standard anatomy.
  • Plausible Glans blood really does drain mainly into the deep dorsal vein, so the "circuit" describes a real pathway. But "bulbo-dorsal circuit" is not a medical term, and the claim that it was "designed to grow the male member" is speculation.
  • Contradicted The source calls the penis "a partially differentiated, non-calcified spongy bone." Many primates have a penile bone (baculum), but humans don't, and human erectile tissue contains no bone. Its spongy structure is vascular: sinusoids lined with endothelium and smooth muscle.
  • Plausible The source says the tunica has one to three genetically variable layers. Anatomy texts describe a fairly consistent two-layer tunica around the CC (inner circular, outer longitudinal). This doesn't change any technique.

The science behind it, checked

How the source explains growth, and where that explanation matches or departs from mainstream physiology.

1 · Shear stress: the trigger

As blood flows along a vessel wall it creates friction, called shear stress. The inner lining (endothelium) is coated with the glycocalyx, a hair-like mesh that senses flow. Faster flow bends these "hairs," and the endothelial cells respond by releasing nitric oxide (NO). NO relaxes the smooth muscle in the vessel wall, and the vessel widens. This is the "pump" you feel in a muscle during exercise, or veins standing out during a hard run.

2 · Arteriogenesis: how vessels grow (the source's account)

Hypoxia vs. shear stress

Low oxygen also triggers vessel growth (angiogenesis), and the source acknowledges that. It calls hypoxia a "panic reaction," though, and treats shear stress as the body's main route to arterial growth. That is why sessions are timed and flow-based rather than taken to oxygen-starved exhaustion. BFR, covered later, deliberately lets the tissue "breathe" at least every 30 seconds.

Practical rules that follow

  • Each session should push flow to the ceiling your vessels can currently handle, and hold it there.
  • Keep moving on rest days (walking, daily activity) so the new channels don't prune.
  • Progress the stimulus as vessels widen. The same flow through a wider vessel produces less shear, which the source calls the "shear threshold."

Evidence check: the science

  • Established Shear stress drives nitric-oxide release, flow-mediated dilation and arteriogenesis. Collateral-artery growth is driven mainly by shear, not by hypoxia (Heil & Schaper 2004). The source's outline of this is broadly right, though details like "VEGF subtype BB" (which mixes up VEGF and PDGF-BB) and the growth-factor half-life figures are not reliable.
  • Community claim No one has measured whether AM1–AM3 or the Wheel produce arteriogenesis in penile tissue.
  • Contradicted "The heart is not a pump" and "the vascular system relies on a vacuum." The heart is the pump of the circulation. Muscle contraction and breathing help return venous blood, but they don't replace the heart. None of the techniques depend on this claim.
  • Contradicted "Smooth muscles relax and contract in response to shear stress, not pressure." Both matter. The pressure-driven Bayliss (myogenic) response is real (Bayliss 1902), and the source relies on it later to justify SABRE.
  • Established Blood keeps flowing during an erection. But veno-occlusive dysfunction ("venous leak") is a real, testable diagnosis (duplex Doppler ultrasound). The community's view that it is usually a misdiagnosis is unsupported, so don't dismiss a urologist's findings.
  • Plausible The idea that sex is the natural template ("use it or lose it") has indirect support: men who had intercourse less than once a week had about twice the ED incidence of men having it weekly over five years (Koskimäki et al. 2008). That is association, not proof that mimicking sex grows tissue.

Foundations

The four pillars

The source is emphatic that the hand techniques are a "polisher" on top of a healthy vascular system, not a replacement for one.

Why this matters (community poll, members training 4+ weeks, N = 94)

Only 26.6% combined consistent cardio with a caloric surplus, and 73.4% were missing at least one of the two. The moderator who ran the poll describes growth as needing four things together: a caloric surplus, a CS stimulus (the Wheel), a CC stimulus (SABRE/BFR), and consistent lower-body cardio of at least 90 minutes a week.

Pillar 1 · Dedicated Lower-body Cardio (DLC)

What & how much

Sustained, leg-driven cardio

  • Examples: recumbent or air bike, stair climber, rower, jump rope, running.
  • Minimum: 90 min per week, typically 30 min × 3 (Mon/Wed/Fri).
  • Intensity: hard enough to raise flow and keep it up. One member trains at about 8/10 effort (≈13 cal/min on an air bike).
  • Janus: 30–45 min on a recumbent bike (while gaming or watching TV), plus a short warm-up of easy bike work or a walk around the block before each session, to "open up the arteries."
Why legs, according to the source

Widen the shared supply lines

Lower-body cardio sends "clouds" of growth factor (VEGF) through the vessels that feed the pelvis. A following Angion session then targets that growth into the penis: "a superhighway from the heart to the tip of the glans."

The source says a leg day with weights isn't enough: intermittent flow (≈150 L per 30 min) doesn't signal that vessels are undersized, while 30 min of steady cardio moves ≈450 L.

Evidence check: cardio

  • Established Aerobic exercise improves erectile function in randomized trials and meta-analyses (Silva et al. 2017). One systematic review suggests about 160 min/week of moderate-to-vigorous aerobic activity for six months for men whose ED is linked to inactivity, obesity, hypertension, metabolic syndrome or cardiovascular disease (Gerbild et al. 2018). Treat the community's 90 min/week as a floor, not a target.
  • Plausible The litre figures are rough whole-body cardiac-output estimates (≈5 L/min at rest, 15+ L/min during hard cardio), not leg-specific measurements.

Pillar 2 · Nutrition & hydration

Source rules

Eat like a bodybuilder

  • Be in a slight caloric surplus with adequate protein during growth phases. The source says this is "non-negotiable" for the Phallogenesis program and required for SABRE/BFR, and warns the penis "can shrink" if you do them at maintenance or in a deficit.
  • When cutting, drop SABRE/BFR. One member cuts Jan–May (187 → 182 lb) and bulks Jun–Dec (182 → 187 lb).
  • Make health-conscious choices: mostly plant-based, low sodium, no heat-damaged oils, avoid dairy, reduce meat, avoid powdered protein formulas.
  • Track processed and total sugar. Spikes in fasting glucose damage vessels and reduce NO signaling.
Before a session

Janus's pre-workout routine

  • Avoid salty, sugary, deep-fried or "heat-damaged oil" meals.
  • Skip caffeine before a session (the source says it constricts penile vessels).
  • Have a light, plant-based meal 20–30 min before: dark leafy greens plus a little oatmeal.
  • Optionally take 5–10 g of L-arginine or L-citrulline 20–30 min before. Janus prefers citrulline for taste.
  • Do 5–10 min of light cardio.
Every day

Water, sodium & potassium

  • Hydrate. The podcast host credits about a gallon a day plus a calm mindset for a fuller flaccid state. That's an anecdote.
  • Track sodium (a vasoconstrictor) against potassium from food (fruit, vegetables, beans and legumes). The source warns against potassium pills because they are easy to overdo.
Supplements mentioned

What members report taking

  • Janus's troubleshooting list: zinc, magnesium, L-citrulline, L-arginine, vitamin D3, vitamin K2.
  • One member: a can of beets daily, 3 tbsp collagen, ½ tsp vitamin C, peanuts.
  • A 4.5-year member: L-citrulline 6 g, L-arginine 2 g, Panax ginseng 350 mg, shilajit, creatine, ashwagandha, CoQ10. He also eats keto in a slight surplus.

Evidence check: nutrition

  • Established Healthy weight, heart-healthy eating and not smoking are among the best-supported ways to improve erections. In one randomized trial, lifestyle change improved ED in obese men (Esposito et al. 2004).
  • Community claim No study shows that a caloric surplus is needed for, or causes, penile growth. Caution: excess body fat raises ED risk and enlarges the pubic fat pad, which visibly shortens the penis. If you follow the surplus advice, keep it small and track your waist.
  • Plausible L-citrulline and L-arginine show modest benefit in mild ED in small trials (Cormio et al. 2011; Rhim et al. 2019). They can lower blood pressure, so check with a doctor if you take nitrates, BP drugs or PDE5 inhibitors.
  • Contradicted Caffeine as a penile vasoconstrictor: in US survey data, men drinking about 2–3 cups of coffee a day had lower odds of ED (Lopez et al. 2015). Skipping coffee before a session is harmless, but it isn't supported as necessary.
  • Established Potassium from food rather than pills is sound advice. Supplements can push potassium dangerously high, especially with kidney disease or some BP drugs.

Pillar 3 · Recovery

Rest days

"Blood vessels need rest just like skeletal muscles." Remodeling happens between sessions. One long-term member's biggest jump came after a vacation forced him to take time off.

Sleep

Nightly and morning erections (NPTR) are the body's own maintenance sessions, and a strong NPTR is a sign your training is dosed correctly.

Stress & mindset

"If you're nervous or squirrelly, it will shrink up." Low stress, confidence and a warm, close relationship all come up repeatedly in long-term success stories.

Pillar 4 · Pelvic floor, position & habits

Pelvic floor

Keep it relaxed

  • Train with a relaxed pelvic floor, and don't use kegels to push blood through a rep. The source says this especially applies to AM2 and is a common cause of a sharp EQ drop or erections that turn "thinner and hard."
  • The source warns that heavy kegeling and edging can overtrain the BS/PC muscles, leaving the IC weak and producing "hard flaccid" and pelvic floor imbalance.
  • One member relieved pelvic tension within days using belly breathing, reverse kegels and deep (Malasana) squats, alongside AM1 and recumbent-bike DLC.
Habits the source flags

Porn, masturbation & more

  • Porn-masturbation-orgasm cycling: the source says it overworks and shortens the pelvic floor muscles. In its account these then "strangle" the chambers, thin the shaft, reduce glans and CS fullness and dull sensitivity, and "death grip" adds vascular trauma. It also claims hand dominance causes curvature: left-hand use leads to a rightward lean, and vice versa.
  • Its advice: no porn. If you masturbate, use the hand techniques instead of the usual method, and keep ejaculation infrequent during training. "If you're not training, keep your dick out of your hands."
  • Alcohol and nicotine: "enormous losses in penile fullness."
  • Clothing: wear loose boxers rather than tight briefs, which the source lists as the third-biggest roadblock to gains.

Evidence check: pelvic floor & habits

  • Established Perineal compression, as studied with bicycle saddles, reduces penile oxygenation and can cause numbness and erectile problems (Sommer et al. 2010). That supports training lying down.
  • Plausible An overly tight (hypertonic) pelvic floor is a recognised contributor to pelvic pain and sexual symptoms, and a pelvic-floor physiotherapist can assess it. Note that pelvic-floor training for weak muscles improved ED in a randomized trial (Dorey et al. 2004). The problem is overuse and tension, not kegels as such.
  • Plausible Pornography and ED: research is mixed. Some survey studies find no robust link (Landripet & Štulhofer 2015), while clinical reports describe men recovering after stopping (Park et al. 2016).
  • Contradicted No evidence shows masturbation shrinks the penis or that hand dominance causes curvature. A curve that develops in adulthood can be Peyronie's disease, so see a urologist.
  • Established Smoking is a strong, well-established ED risk factor, and heavy drinking impairs erections. Avoiding ejaculation is a training preference, not a health need: higher ejaculation frequency has been linked to lower prostate-cancer risk (Rider et al. 2016).

Measuring progress

The source's main gauge is erection quality, tracked daily. Size measurements come second.

The EQ scale

The community rates erection quality (EQ) subjectively from 1 to 10 and treats a falling EQ as the sign of overtraining. To make your numbers repeatable, anchor them to the clinically validated Erection Hardness Score (EHS, 1–4) (Mulhall et al. 2007). The mapping below is a suggested convention, not part of the source.

EHSClinical descriptionSuggested EQ (1–10)
1Penis is larger, but not hard1–3
2Hard, but not hard enough for penetration4–5
3Hard enough for penetration, but not completely hard6–8
4Completely hard and fully rigid9–10

Daily markers the source tells you to watch

Good signs

  • Morning and nightly erections (NPTR) return or get stronger.
  • Post-session fullness lasts 10–30 min, which tells you the dose was right.
  • A heavier, warmer, "spongy" flaccid hang and fewer "cold flaccids."
  • Post-meal fullness after a health-conscious meal.
  • Visible vascularity, better colour, and "vaso-pumps" (temporary post-session size increases).
  • A dorsal artery pulse you can feel, and later a member that bobs with your heartbeat.

How to check the dorsal pulse (graduation test for AM1)

  1. With a full erection, lying down, rest two fingers lightly on top of the shaft, one on each side of the centre vein (Figure 1).
  2. Press very gently. Pressing hard blocks the pulse.
  3. Note whether it's absent, faint or easy to feel. "Easily palpable" is the AM1 → AM2 criterion.

By the end of AM2, the dorsal arteries become visible when heavily engorged. In AM3 they take on a "rope-like" look.

After a session, the visible arteries may look larger while the pulse feels the same or even weaker. Janus reads this as remodeling in progress: "blood vessels... too can experience a pump."

Measuring size (if you track it)

  • Same conditions every time: full EQ, same time of day, a warm room, the same method. Measure monthly at most.
  • Take three readings and record the median. Erect length changes by several millimetres with arousal and temperature, so changes under about 0.5 cm (0.2″) may just be noise.
  • Know the "newbie gain": the source notes that "most guys grow about ¼″ in length and girth just due to improved EQ alone," because a harder erection measures bigger.

Context worth knowing

In a pooled analysis of more than 15,000 men, the average erect length was about 13.1 cm (5.2″) and the average erect girth about 11.7 cm (4.6″) (Veale et al. 2015). Many men who worry about size fall within the normal range. Several long-term members also say their partners cared far more about hardness, closeness and confidence than about measurements.

Two ways the source explains the ladder

Why you shouldn't skip stages

According to the creator, most men present with arterial insufficiency unless they are already very well endowed (girth above about 5.5″ or length above about 7″), and "no tissue can outpace its base nutrient delivery rates." A small vessel can only pass so much blood per minute, "and it matters little how much a male might struggle to supersede that limit." Each stage is designed to push flow up to your current ceiling and hold it there for the session without outstripping inflow.

  • Losing some fullness is a good sign: you're stimulating adequately.
  • Losing the erection means you're outstripping inflow, so back off.

…and why you shouldn't stall

"The opposite of crazy is still crazy." Once you've conquered a stage, move up. Use "taste-testing": try a few minutes of the next technique at the end of a session. If you can do it, you're ready. A 2-on / 1-off schedule is the one exception the source allows, briefly, to bridge a gap before graduating.

Stage finder

Answer in order. This runs entirely in your browser and nothing is saved.

1. Can you get an erection without medication, injections or a pump?

2. When erect, can you easily feel a pulse in your dorsal arteries (two fingers resting lightly either side of the centre vein)?

3. Can you do 20–30 minutes of AM2 without the underside (CS) going flat?

4. When you try AM3 (Vascion), can you keep going for at least 5 minutes before the CS flattens?

5. Can you complete 30 minutes of AM3, including 10–15 minutes at high speed, without losing fullness?

Stage E · "E class"

Angio pumping

Evidence check: SABRE

  • Established The Bayliss (myogenic) response is real: smooth muscle contracts when suddenly stretched (Bayliss 1902).
  • Community claim No study shows that striking the penis produces beneficial remodeling. The closest clinical analogue is low-intensity shockwave therapy (Li-ESWT), which delivers calibrated energy from a medical device. Trials in vasculogenic ED show modest, mixed benefit (Clavijo et al. 2017), and it isn't comparable to manual or tool strikes. If the idea interests you, ask a urologist about Li-ESWT.
  • Plausible The injury mechanisms in the warning box above (fracture, Peyronie's, vessel rupture) are well documented for blunt penile trauma.

B · BFR: blood-flow-restriction training ("arterialization")

What the source says it does. Veins have the least smooth muscle and supporting matrix of any vessel, so they are the most sensitive to pressure. Research on vein grafts shows that a vein moved into the high-pressure arterial system starts to "arterialize": it builds smooth muscle and collagen. The source argues that briefly pressurising the penis's venous networks does something similar. Stretched endothelial cells sprout and release growth factors (PDGF) that "call out" to smooth muscle (Janus's "star-crossed lovers" image). Smooth muscle then migrates out toward the veins, and new capillary beds form. Because new channels "prune" without flow ("use them or lose them"), BFR has to be combined with shear-based work (AM/Wheel). The source notes that stretch-based effects show up hours after a session, while shear-based effects are immediate.

Community framing: glans pulsing works the CS, and cyclic bending works the CC. BFR "cements" the gains made by the Wheel (CS) and SABRE (CC).

Cyclic bending (source instructions)

  1. While heavily engorged, clamp down on the base of the penis with one hand.
  2. With the other hand, take an overhand grip on the upper portion, with the thumb pointing back toward the body, not away.
  3. Keeping the clamp in place, kegel in a bit of blood.
  4. Gently and cyclically bend to the left or right. Blood tries to leave the internal spaces but the clamp stops it, and "as pressure mounts when you start to bend, it will cause local network-wide vascular expansion, from the arterial networks all the way up to the point of flow restriction."

Glans pulsing (source instructions)

  1. Grip the base, this time heavily erect, near or at 100%, so outward flow is restricted.
  2. With the other hand, grip the glans and give it gentle pulsing squeezes. "Keyword here: gentle!" The aim is to pulse the venous networks with pressure changes, not to burst anything.
  3. A dull soreness may build as you continue. The source calls a little aching a sign you're adequately stressing the tissue, but never train to pain, red spots or burst vessels: "all of those things are a big no-go."

Dose and rules

  • Total: a cumulative 15–20 min of cyclic bending plus glans pulsing (Janus's current best window).
  • Frequency: 1-on / 1-off to 1-on / 2-off, depending on age and food intake.
  • Order: best done in the same session as the Angion Methods or Wheel, after them.
  • Breathe: let up the squeezes after no more than 30 seconds. "We are not trying to induce a hypoxic state... and no it will not be beneficial to try."

Overtraining & troubleshooting

"Overtraining is a very real thing." Its main sign is a drop in erection quality, so let your daily EQ log guide you.

Signs the dose is right

  • You stay erect with little effort: no stopping to re-stimulate, no need to think about sex the whole time.
  • Post-session fullness lasts at least 10 and up to 30 minutes.
  • Good flaccid hang for the rest of the day.
  • Strong morning wood (NPTR).
  • Visible post-session "vaso-pumps."

Signs you did too much

  • Hard to stay erect during the session.
  • The erection disappears right after the session.
  • A small, shrivelled, "turtled" flaccid hang.
  • Loss of morning wood, and EQ falling over several days.
  • Pain, tenderness or red spots. Stop and rest.

The community explains the mechanism this way: push past what your vessels can currently support and "your body will try to limit the amount of blood that can go through your member," which shows up as vasoconstriction. The usual trap is that results arrive, men move from 1-on / 1-off to 2-on / 1-off "thinking that will give them even better results," and EQ drops instead.

The fix, in order

  1. Add rest. Go to 1-on / 2-off. "If you let your EQ be your guide... it will help you know if you're overtraining." One member's example: EQ dipped on 2-on / 1-off AM2. He went back to 1-on / 1-off AM1 for a week, and EQ "came right back." Another member's biggest gains came right after a forced week off.
  2. Lower the intensity or the time. Drop one level (AM3 → AM2, or rod strikes → thumb strikes) or shorten the session (for example, 15 → 10 minutes).
  3. Check the pillars (§05): calories and protein, cardio, sleep, porn and ejaculation frequency, alcohol and nicotine.
  4. Check your position. Groin or testicle pain usually stops once you train lying down. If it continues while lying down, it's usually overtraining. If it continues after rest, see a doctor.

Plateaus, thresholds & sensitivity

The shear threshold

As an artery widens, the same flow produces less shear. "In order to make an artery become larger, it is imperative to clear the shear threshold," so progress the technique, speed or device. A brief 2-on / 1-off can bridge to the next stage, then go back to 1-on / 1-off.

Don't skip ahead

Members who jump straight to the Wheel, SABRE or BFR often lose the erection or see EQ drop. "It took me MONTHS of work" to get good sessions from the Wheel and from rod strikes. "The right movement for you is the one that gives you the best workout."

Sensitivity & ejaculation

Reports differ. Some members find themselves more sensitive (the source says VEGF also promotes nerve growth). Others report much better ejaculatory control. Either way: pause when you're close, breathe, keep the pelvic floor relaxed.
